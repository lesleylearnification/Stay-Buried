THE CHALLENGE

Most compliance training is boring, boring, boring. Read some text and click next. Choose from a bunch of options of which one is obviously correct. Click next. Repeat. Repeat. Repeat.

STAY BURIED was designed to provide practice at recognizing cues that actually matter, knowing what action and escalation path fits the situation, and retrieving the same underlying principle later when the situation no longer resembles the example that taught it. The design challenge was to turn those invisible judgment processes into things the player could physically do, see, get wrong, and learn from.

GAME FANTASY

You are a Feline Memory Retriever. The Buried has stolen your memories and hidden them inside unresolved cases. They are located in a haunted Archive which includes an apartment, an after-hours veterinary clinic, and a pre-dawn animal sanctuary.

Strong judgment restores your memory, while judgment gives The Buried an opening. Invisible organizational risk becomes visible based on what you resolve, mishandle, or leave exposed. You only have one night to recover the memories while keep The Buried buried until sunrise.

LEARNING OBJECTIVE

Given three realistic compliance scenarios, correctly identify the compliance situation, route it to the appropriate person or process, and make a retention-ready decision under pressure with at least 80% optimal choices.

GAME GOAL

Recover the memories The Buried stole and keep it buried until sunrise.

GAMEPLAY

The three scenes deliberately do not ask the learner to repeat the same interaction.

In Scene 1, THE APARTMENT THAT REMEMBERS, the player searches the environment for four meaningful clues. A senior manager has a legitimate business need, but customer records are ready to be exported to a personal Gmail account. The player has to separate authority and business need from the risky method being used, reflect in the field journal, and commit to a response.

In Scene 2, THE CLINIC AFTER CLOSING, recognition becomes action. The player receives a confidential concern and physically builds a three-step handoff from six possible actions. The safest sequence preserves the facts and timing, explains the limits of confidentiality, and uses the designated after-hours response process. The route itself is the assessment; there is no redundant multiple-choice question afterward.

Scene 3, THE SANCTUARY OF BORROWED NAMES, tests transfer. The surface details change. The player searches a pre-dawn animal sanctuary, discovers evidence embedded in the actual environment, recovers it, and builds two evidence packages. Four of five recovered items must be combined into two relationships, leaving one unused. The player then routes both packages to the process or person they believe owns the response.

Crucially, the interface does not prevent a bad decision. Two items in each package is enough to continue, whether the reasoning is excellent or disastrous. Both packages routed is enough to seal the response. Only then does the game evaluate what the learner actually constructed. Completion gates are structural; learning evaluation is consequential.

Every scene changes MEMORY RESTORED and the relationship between the Memory Retriever and The Buried. The final field journal reconstructs the learner’s actual decisions, routes, package choices, unused evidence, and consequences before asking the transfer question underneath the entire prototype: “When the surface details change, what will tell you that the same underlying risk is present?”

MOTIVATION

AUTONOMY: The learner is allowed to make consequential choices. The game does not quietly correct a bad evidence package or block a poor route before commitment. The player owns the decision.

COMPETENCE / MASTERY: The challenge progresses from recognizing evidence, to constructing an ordered response, to transferring the principle into a new situation that looks different. Feedback diagnoses the reasoning after commitment, so failure can teach rather than simply reject an answer.

RELATEDNESS: The player is not positioned as “Employee 47 completing compliance training.” They inhabit the role of a Retriever recovering something personally meaningful that has been stolen from them. The Cat, Dog, and Alien paths are designed as distinct identities and worlds rather than cosmetic avatars.

IMMERSION: The haunted Archive, changing night environments, Memory Restored meter, Black Marks, The Buried, and the approach of sunrise turn otherwise abstract organizational consequences into visible changes in the game world. The compliance mechanics are part of the fiction rather than questions pasted on top of it.

GAME INSPIRATION

STAY BURIED draws from investigative and environmental games more than conventional training games. Return of the Obra Dinn influenced the idea that the player should reconstruct meaning from evidence rather than be told what to notice. Her Story influenced the pleasure of discovering significance through fragments. I’m on Observation Duty informed the act of carefully reading an environment for meaningful anomalies. Papers, Please influenced the tension between rules, context, human pressure, and consequential judgment.

The goal was not to imitate any of those games mechanically. It was to borrow their trust in the player: put evidence in front of them, give the decision weight, and let the system respond to what they actually do.

REFLECTION

The biggest development issue with this game was getting scene 3 to work. Despite having created a “Source of Truth” game dev document, AI kept creating a hidden-hotspot repeat of scene 1. The trick, I’ve found, is getting AI to assess every way in which a part of a game fails to align with the Source of Truth, and then create a prompt to fix it. Another annoying AI trait is that if you ask it if it has suggestions, it keeps saying that it does. The other trick is to instruct it to “lock in” any fixes and any additional suggestions and improvements, to include those in the prompt. Once it delivers a prompt, then instruct it to build.

As games that have more involved mechanics can end up in a weird loop of endless iteration, I’ve instructed AI to go back through our entire chat, and provide me with a succinct, bulleted-list of every mistake it made, and provide me with a second bulleted list of how this will be avoided in the future. Then “locking this in” as a framework has definitely proved helpful in developing subsequent games.
